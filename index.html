Narrative-driven video game

Peaceland

Role
UX/UI Design
Narrative Team
What I did

Duration
Jan 2026 – Jul 2026
Tools
Figma, Adobe Photoshop, Cinema4D
Mentors
Dr. Marcia Hartwell, Prof. Kristana Textor

A narrative video game about a country 30 years after a civil war.

Players relive survivors’ memories in a museum, and their choices shape its future.

How can a game help young people move past divisions from a war they never lived through?

We developed it in three directions: the player experience, an authentic look built from real references, and research on inherited bias. By the end, players had switched from the character most like them to another, they called the world “very authentic,” and the research became a Schwartz Discover Scholar project with a team to carry it on.

Reflection

Reflection

What I learned

I learned that bias is taught, and that design taste can be developed and adjusted to each project. Until Peaceland, I had been far too comfortable in my own art and design style. This project expanded the limits of what I can create collaboratively, and of what I’m able to achieve.

What I’d change next

After the playtests, I’d change how the game is presented to players and add more language options. I’d also make the consequences of each choice more visible, add more memories and real-life scenarios, and playtest with a bigger group, including other parts of the world.

Where Peaceland stands

After two years of research, three summers of onboarding and a fully playtested Romeo & Juliet memory, Peaceland has a clear design goal and a team structure built to sustain it, and it is open to more undergraduate researchers. The biggest wins were art, story and authenticity; the biggest open work is pacing, decision-making and minigame depth.

01Overview

Soulshine is a desk lamp and companion app that gently pulls people out of mindless scrolling. Instead of adding another screen, it uses calm light, sound, scent and vibration to bring your attention back.

I led our team of four as Team Lead and Visual Designer. I ran our workflow and timeline, developed the user personas, and shaped the design vision. I also designed and built the Soulshine website you can explore above, and created its logo and brand.

02Challenge

The problem

Scrolling damages focus, disrupts sleep, and harms mental health. We grounded the project in 11 academic sources on short-form content, attention span, memory and wellbeing, then surveyed 31 students and young professionals.

Survey

71%say scrolling has hurt their ability to focus

Survey

74%have lost track of time while scrolling

Survey

61%want a physical device to remind them to stop

The gap we found

What already exists

Apps and lockboxes

Forest, One Sec, Opal and physical phone lockboxes each tackle part of the problem, either on the screen or by locking the phone away.

What was missing

Physical and digital, together

None of them combine physical and digital signals into one calm experience, even though research shows paired interventions work best. That gap is what Soulshine fills.

03Process

VALS

01Overview

VALS is a browser extension that reads Terms & Conditions for you. It scans any legal agreement and plays back a clear spoken summary in your language, so you know what you are agreeing to before you click Accept.

I was the Product Designer and Front-End Developer on a team of five. I built the complete VALS website you can explore above, including its UI/UX, animations, payment flow and navigation, and prototyped the product itself in Figma with user flows, wireframes and high-fidelity screens. I also designed the VALS logo, first in Adobe Photoshop and then as a 3D model in Figma.

02Challenge

The problem

Terms & Conditions decide what happens to our data, rights and privacy, yet they are built to be unreadable.

Deloitte, 2017

91%of people accept terms without reading them

Average agreement

10,000+words, which takes 30 to 40 minutes to read

The Atlantic, 2012

73days a year to read every policy we agree to

Barrier 1

Length and complexity

Most people don't have half an hour to read one agreement, so they skip it.

Barrier 2

Language

Agreements come in only a handful of languages, leaving billions of non-native speakers in the dark.

Barrier 3

Accessibility

Text-only documents exclude visually impaired, dyslexic and elderly users, and screen readers can't simplify legal language.

Who it's for

2.2 billion people

Visually impaired

Screen readers can't simplify dense legal text. A voice-first design removes that barrier.

15–20% have dyslexia

Reading difficulties

Audio and plain-language summaries replace impenetrable paragraphs, for the 130 million US adults who struggle with literacy too.

761 million aged 65+

Elderly users

Small print gets harder to read with age. Voice guidance feels like a familiar phone call.

03Process

How VALS came to be

VALS started in one of our team’s brainstorming sessions. One of my teammates has a visually impaired family member, and we wanted to make something that would help them.

  1. Brainstorm

    One person we wanted to help

    A teammate’s visually impaired family member gave us our starting point.

  2. Widen

    Three groups who struggle with the fine print

    We widened the goal to visually impaired people, elderly users and people with reading difficulties.

  3. Design

    A product that reads it for you

    We designed a product for anyone who struggles to use products and read the “fine print” in today’s very technological world.

04Final design

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Install

    Add VALS to Chrome, Edge or Firefox in one click. It sits quietly in the toolbar.

  2. Step 2

    Browse normally

    VALS scans each page in the background, and its icon lights up the moment it finds Terms of Service, a privacy policy or any legal agreement.

  3. Step 3

    Hear your summary

    Click the icon, or turn on Auto Mode, and VALS reads the agreement back as a spoken summary in your language in under two minutes.

  4. Step 4

    Decide with confidence

    VALS rates the risk as low, medium or high and flags the clauses that matter, such as data sharing, arbitration and cancellation rights.

About

Hi, I'm Ana!

I'm a multidisciplinary designer double majoring in Computer Science and Digital Media Studies at the University of Rochester, with a focus in Psychology. I love complex problems, especially ones that call for creative thinking, and I feel most at home where logic, design, and human needs meet.

My work spans UX/UI, graphic design, and human-computer interaction. At Peaceland Research, I took an interactive game from concept to prototype and playtested it across 4 international regions. On the iZone's graphic design team, I create social media graphics and campaigns, including for a TEDx event. I've also designed Soulshine, a digital wellbeing website, and VALS, an AI voice assistant that turns documents into audio summaries in 6 languages.

I believe technology should make life easier and more accessible, and I'm especially curious about how people think, play, and interact. Most of my inspiration comes from the people and the nature around me. People remind me who I'm designing for. Nature shows me that beauty and purpose can be the same thing, since every curve of a canyon wall and every vein in a leaf is there for a reason.

As a designer, my goal is to create work that is both beautiful and genuinely useful: products that are easy and accessible for everyone, and stories and games that bring people closer together. I look for every chance to learn from professionals in the field as I shape my direction.

Feel free to look around my projects, and you can find my resume, LinkedIn, or a direct link to email me right below. I'd love to connect, so reach out!

Duality

Mural · Cultural Showcase

For the Cultural Showcase at Armand Hammer United World College, themed Duality, I designed and painted a wall-sized mural. To find its symbols, I interviewed 15 people from different regions and countries, including the United States, Peru, Italy, Egypt, Turkey, Syria, Japan, Namibia, Oman, Senegal, Ukraine, Bangladesh and Thailand, about what duality means to them and which symbols their cultures use for it. Then I brought those symbols together into one piece, from the yin and yang in the eyes to the patterns around the two faces.

The mural took me a month to paint. I also worked with the technical stage team so that the colors, and what the stage lights picked out, worked with each of the different performances.

What I learned: how to conduct design interviews. It was crucial to get reliable information from real people, the real viewers of my work.
